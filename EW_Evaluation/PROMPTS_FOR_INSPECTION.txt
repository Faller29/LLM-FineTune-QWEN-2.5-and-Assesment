THE MOORBURG LETTER
230 Sycamore Street
Moorburg
April 10
Dear Editor:
Overnight parking on all streets in Moorburg should be eliminated. To achieve this goal, parking should be prohibited from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m There are a number of reasons why any intelligent citizen should agree.
1. For one thing, to park overnight is to have a garage in the streets. Now it is illegal for anyone to have a garage in the city streets. Clearly, then, it should be against the law to park overnight in the streets.
2. Three important streets, Lincoln Avenue, Marquand Avenue, and West Main Street, are very narrow. With cars parked on the streets, there really isn't room for the heavy traffic that passes over them in the afternoon rush hour. When driving home in the afternoon after work, it takes me thirty-five minutes to make a trip that takes ten minutes during the uncrowded time. If there were no cars parked on the side of these streets, they could handle considerably more traffic.
3. Traffic on some streets is also bad in the morning when factory workers are on their way to the 6 a.m. shift. If there were no cars parked on these streets between 2 a.m. and 6 a.m., then there would be more room for this traffic.
4. Furthermore, there can be no doubt that, in general, overnight parking on the streets is undesirable. It is definitely bad and should be opposed.
5. If parking is prohibited from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m., then accidents between parked and moving vehicles will be nearly eliminated during this period. All intelligent citizens would regard the near elimination of accidents in any period as highly desirable. So, we should be in favor of prohibiting parking from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m.
6. Last month, the Chief of Police, Burgess Jones, ran an experiment which proves that parking should be prohibited from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. On one of our busiest streets, Marquand Avenue, he placed experimental signs for one day. The signs prohibited parking from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. During the four-hour period, there was not one accident on Marquand. Everyone knows, of course, that there have been over four hundred accidents on Marquand during the past year.
7. The opponents of my suggestions have said that conditions are safe enough now. These people don't know what "safe" really means. Conditions are not safe if there's even the slightest possible chance for an accident. That's what "safe" means. So, conditions are not safe the way they are now.
8. Finally, let me point out that the Director of the National Traffic Safety Council, Kenneth O. Taylor, has strongly recommended that overnight street parking be prevented on busy streets in cities the size of Moorburg. The National Association of Police Chiefs has made the same recommendation. Both suggest that prohibiting parking from 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. is the best way to prevent overnight parking.
I invite those who disagree, as well as those who agree with me, to react to my letter through the editor of this paper. Let's get this issue out in the open.
Sincerely, 
Robert R. Raywift

(1) Xiao Wu runs a clothing store in a commercial district. Recently, sales have been unsatisfactory. During store inspections, Xiao Wu noticed that when there are many customers, staff cannot provide timely service. He speculated that delayed service might be causing customers to lose their desire to purchase. To prove his hypothesis, he randomly interviewed 6 customers who entered the store (4 female customers and 2 male customers). Two female customers and one male customer said that delayed service would indeed "dampen" their desire to purchase, while the remaining customers indicated that service timing did not affect their shopping. Therefore, Xiao Wu concluded that female customers care more about timely service than male customers, so he instructed staff to prioritize serving female customers.

(2) Additionally, Xiao Wu renovated the store because he felt that selecting clothes in a pleasing environment would increase customers' desire to purchase. Sales increased by 25% compared to the week before renovation, and Xiao Wu was very satisfied with this result, believing it proved his renovation decision was completely correct.

(3) Finally, Xiao Wu recalled that last summer, the store adopted an off-season sales strategy, selling stockpiled leather jackets, wool coats, and down jackets at a discount between June and August, achieving a profit of 100,000 yuan. Based on this, Xiao Wu decided to implement the off-season sales strategy again this winter, selling stockpiled summer clothing at a discount between November and January. He believes this will create another profit peak, achieving or even exceeding 100,000 yuan in profit.

(1) There are increasingly more people riding electric bicycles, leading to crowded non-motorized vehicle lanes. Electric bicycles often cross the boundaries of non-motorized lanes into motorized vehicle lanes, which is very dangerous. The busy city center already experiences frequent traffic accidents - in the past year alone, there were over 100 traffic accidents in the city center. If electric bicycles could be banned from these streets, the number of accidents in the city center would greatly decrease.
(2) An internet user compiled statistics on the number of traffic accidents in our city center and the purchase volume of electric bicycles by our city residents over the past three years, finding that both have been increasing year by year. This shows that the increase in electric bicycles has indeed led to worse traffic conditions, making residents' travel less safe, so it is necessary to restrict electric bicycles from entering the city center.
(3) Electric bicycles themselves pose safety risks. We've all heard news about electric bicycles causing fires while charging in residential buildings. One could say that an electric bicycle battery while charging is no different from a bomb that could explode at any time. Having these "bombs" in the crowded, bustling city center could have unimaginable consequences if danger occurs. Therefore, I strongly recommend banning electric bicycles on busy streets in the city center.
